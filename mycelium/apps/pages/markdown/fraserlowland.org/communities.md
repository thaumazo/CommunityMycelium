# 🏘️ Communities of the Fraser Lowland

*Networks of people caring for place.*

---

## **🌿 What We Mean by “Community”**

In the Fraser Lowland, community is not defined only by boundaries, institutions, or legal structures.

It includes:

* **Grassroots collectives** — informal groups organizing around land, water, justice, food, art, or mutual aid.
* **Neighbourhood & place-based groups** — villages, watersheds, eco-communities, block networks, houseless communities, rural towns.
* **Cultural, faith-based, and Indigenous communities** — carrying stories, stewardship practices, ceremony, and continuity across generations.
* **Nonprofits, co-ops, alliances, and local organizations** — working on food systems, climate resilience, education, housing, disaster relief, and more.
* **Learning circles, maker groups, guilds, and affinity networks** — where shared practice, craft, or purpose brings people together.

All of these are part of the living fabric of the bioregion.

---

## **🤲 Why This Page Exists**

Communities are where regeneration truly happens — not only in ideas or policy, but in relationships, shared work, and local action.

This page helps us:

* **See the tapestry of groups across the bioregion**
* **Find partnerships, avoid duplication, and support each other’s work**
* **Share what each community needs and what it can offer**
* **Strengthen the connections between upstream and downstream, urban and rural, human and more-than-human**

## **💚 Principles We Honour**

* **Respect for autonomy & consent** — each group defines itself.
* **Indigenous sovereignty & leadership** — we acknowledge this land remains unceded and stewarded since time immemorial.
* **Equity & inclusion** — especially centering frontline, marginalized, and historically excluded communities.
* **Relational over transactional** — partnerships built on trust, reciprocity, and shared purpose.
* **Many paths, one bioregion** — we celebrate diversity of approach while holding a shared responsibility to place.
