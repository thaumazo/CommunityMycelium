# Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

<hr>

## Understanding the Process

<details>
  <summary>How does Localism work?</summary>

  <p>Localism unfolds as <strong>three expanding circles</strong>:</p>

  <ol>
    <li>
      <p><strong>The Community (Everyone Welcome)</strong><br>
      Anyone interested can join our Discord space with access to online gatherings, shared resources, and connection with others doing related work across the Fraser Lowland.</p>
    </li>
    <li>
      <p><strong>The Intensives (32 Participants)</strong><br>
      From the broader community, stewards invite up to 32 people into deeper facilitated sessions throughout February. These sessions include collaborative workshops, developing project ideas together, and practicing shared decision-making.</p>
    </li>
    <li>
      <p><strong>The Funded Projects (6–8 Projects)</strong><br>
      The 32 participants use collaborative decision-making to choose 6–8 projects that receive $2,500 each for one-month projects in March.</p>
    </li>
  </ol>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>What does collaborative decision-making mean?</summary>

  <p>Instead of a panel deciding who gets funding, the 32 participants work together to choose which projects receive support. Everyone has a voice in shaping how resources flow.</p>

  <p>We use a process called <strong>sociocratic selection</strong>, where decisions are made based on whether anyone has a principled objection, rather than requiring everyone to agree.</p>

  <p>Learn more: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociocracy</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>What happens in the Discord community space?</summary>

  <p>Discord is where participants connect informally, share updates, ask questions, find collaborators, and access resources. Think of it as a digital gathering space where you can participate as much or as little as feels right.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>What are the eight domains?</summary>

  <p>Each participant's work connects to at least one of these areas:</p>

  <ul>
    <li>Building resilient systems</li>
    <li>Creating interconnected communities</li>
    <li>Fostering shared knowledge</li>
    <li>Cultivating cultural belonging</li>
    <li>Regenerating ecosystems</li>
    <li>Ensuring fair access to resources</li>
    <li>Aligning technology with community values</li>
    <li>Practicing collaborative leadership</li>
  </ul>

  <p>You can learn more about the stewards holding each domain on our <strong>Stewards</strong> page.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Can I work across multiple domains?</summary>

  <p>Yes. Many changemakers work across multiple areas, and your work doesn't need to fit neatly into one category.</p>
</details>

## Understanding Our Approach

<details>
  <summary>What values guide this work?</summary>

  <p>Localism is shaped by how we work together, not just what we do. These values guide the community:</p>

  <p><strong>Regeneration over extraction</strong><br>
  We aim to leave people, places, and relationships more resourced than we found them. This means resisting dynamics that lead to burnout or strip value for short-term gain.</p>

  <p><strong>Transparency and trust</strong><br>
  We're clear about how decisions are made, how resources flow, and what's still emerging. This helps reduce confusion and build trust.</p>

  <p><strong>Consent and care in storytelling</strong><br>
  Participation never requires sharing more than you're comfortable with. We ask before recording or sharing stories, and honor personal and cultural boundaries.</p>

  <p><strong>Learning over proving</strong><br>
  This is a learning space. We value honest questions over confident answers, and treat adaptation as part of the work—not as failure.</p>

  <p><strong>Relationship before outcomes</strong><br>
  Strong relationships make better projects and more resilient communities. When we face choices between speed and care, we lean toward what sustains relationship.</p>
</details>

## About Participating

<details>
  <summary>What if I'm not sure I'm "qualified"?</summary>

  <p>Many of the most important changemakers don't think of themselves as "leaders." If you're doing meaningful work and care about your community, you belong here.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>What if I don't have a specific project in mind?</summary>

  <p>That's fine. You don't need to have everything figured out. The process helps you develop and refine ideas in community.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Can I apply even if someone nominated me?</summary>

  <p>Yes. A nomination opens the door, and you're also welcome to apply yourself. Many people do both.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Can I nominate someone AND apply myself?</summary>

  <p>Absolutely. Both doors are open to you.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>What if I'm too busy?</summary>

  <p>You choose your level of participation. Some people join the broader community and show up when they can. Others go deeper into the intensives. You decide what works for your life right now.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>How are the 32 participants selected?</summary>

  <p>A diverse group of community stewards reviews applications and nominations, looking for people who would meaningfully benefit from and contribute to deeper engagement.</p>

  <p>Selection happens across the eight domains, with each domain steward inviting approximately <strong>4 participants</strong> whose work aligns with their area.</p>

  <p>Learn more about our stewards on the <strong>Stewards</strong> page.</p>
</details>

## Practical Details

<details>
  <summary>What kind of time commitment is involved?</summary>

  <p><strong>Broader community:</strong> A few hours a month — join gatherings when you can</p>

  <p><strong>Intensives (32 participants):</strong> Several full or half-day sessions in February, plus ongoing engagement (approximately <strong>20–30 hours total</strong> across the month)</p>

  <p><strong>Funded projects (6–8 projects):</strong> Active project work in March plus documentation (varies by project)</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Do I need to attend all the February sessions to be considered for the 32?</summary>

  <p>We're looking for meaningful engagement, not perfect attendance. Life happens. If you're invited to the intensives, we'll work with you to participate in ways that honor your circumstances.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Where will in-person sessions be held?</summary>

  <p>Sessions will be held at accessible locations within the Fraser Lowland, likely in the Greater Vancouver area. Exact locations will be shared with invited participants.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>What if I can't get to in-person sessions?</summary>

  <p>We're committed to accessibility. If transportation, mobility, or other factors make in-person participation challenging, reach out to us. We'll explore what's possible together.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Are there costs to participate?</summary>

  <p>No. Participation is free. If transportation or other costs would prevent you from participating, please reach out — we'll figure something out together.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Do I have to commit to everything?</summary>

  <p>No. You choose your level of participation based on what feels right for you and what your life allows.</p>
</details>

## About Funding

<details>
  <summary>How are the 6–8 funded projects chosen?</summary>

  <p>The 32 participants work together using collaborative decision-making to choose which projects receive funding. It's a peer-led process, not top-down selection.</p>

  <p>Participants consider each project based on shared criteria and potential impact for the region.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>What if I don't receive project funding?</summary>

  <p>You're still part of the community. The connections you make, the learning you experience, and the relationships you build have value regardless of whether your specific project receives funding.</p>

  <p>Many participants find that the community support, collaborative learning, and network they gain open other doors for their work.</p>
</details>

## For Nominators

<details>
  <summary>What is a nomination, really?</summary>

  <p>A nomination is an invitation, a signal of trust, a door being opened.</p>

  <p>It is <strong>NOT</strong>:</p>
  <ul>
    <li>An endorsement of a specific project</li>
    <li>A ranking or competition</li>
    <li>Required for participation</li>
  </ul>

  <p>You're helping us find people who might not step forward on their own.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>How much detail do I need to provide in a nomination?</summary>

  <p>We're not asking for long essays. A few sentences about who they are, what they do, and why you think they'd be a good fit is plenty.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Who should I nominate?</summary>

  <p>Nominate people who are:</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Doing meaningful community work (paid or unpaid)</li>
    <li>Rooted in the Fraser Lowland</li>
    <li>Someone who might not think of themselves as a "leader"</li>
    <li>Working with care and showing up with integrity</li>
  </ul>

  <p>If they're doing good work and would benefit from community connection, they're a good fit.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>What if the person I'm thinking of is already well-supported?</summary>

  <p>If they're doing meaningful work and would benefit from connecting with others across the region, nominate them. This community is about connection and collaboration, not just filling gaps.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>What if they're too busy?</summary>

  <p>Let them decide. They can choose how much to participate. Even connecting with a few others doing similar work might be valuable to them.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>What if I'm not sure they're "good enough"?</summary>

  <p>Nominate them. We're looking for people who are trying and showing up, not people who have it all figured out. Your sense that they're doing meaningful work is what matters.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Can I nominate more than one person?</summary>

  <p>Yes. Nominate as many people as feels right.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Will they know I nominated them?</summary>

  <p>Yes. We share this in the invitation we send to them.</p>

  <p>If you would prefer to remain anonymous, reach out to us directly and we can discuss. We aim for transparency whenever possible.</p>
</details>

<details>
  <summary>Can they apply themselves instead of being nominated?</summary>

  <p>Yes. Anyone can apply directly. Nominations help us find people who might not apply on their own, but both pathways are open.</p>
</details>
